## Core takeaways

- Current cloud cost solutions offer scattered insights; it can be a challenge to see cloud spend in real time, let alone per pipeline.
- This is an unsustainable path for a rapidly growing industry with accelerating cloud expenditure.

## The Hidden Cost Crisis

In the pharma and bioinformatics space, cloud costs are rising fast. And the real culprit isn’t AWS or GCP. Teams are misallocating resources not out of negligence, but because they lack visibility. Without knowing where the money goes, expense management is impossible.

Cloud infrastructure has become the default foundation over on-premises for managing software on high-performance computing platforms. 

Its scalability, collaboration potential and perceived cost-effectiveness have driven widespread integration; a trend that will only accelerate with increasing AI adoption. 

Yet, companies are facing unpredictable bills, spending between $10 and $100 million on cloud resources annually, that they cannot attribute or optimize.  

This is not sustainable in the long run for an industry that is predicted to triple in size by 2032.

## The biggest black box in the computational budget

Poor attribution is driven by a lack of visibility into cloud spend. Current cloud cost observability providers do not tailor their insights to the complexity of the sector.  
 
Bioinformatics workloads are inherently erratic, with unpredictable cost spikes and complex configurations that are difficult to monitor. Cloud optimization tools need to recognize this, adapt, and provide granular insight.  

Instead, the status quo is that each provider, e.g., AWS Cloudwatch, offers visibility into one piece of the cloud spend puzzle, failing to capture the complete picture.  

As a result, scientific computing is left in the dark: there is little understanding of how cloud resources are consumed or where money is actually flowing.

<Quote>
“We observe costs by jumping into people's accounts and checking the numbers ourselves, or we don't observe at all. That's the bar right now.”
</Quote>
This makes it nearly impossible to rightsize workloads or understand actual cloud usage, which leads to inefficient cloud budgets that produce waste.

## Do-It-Yourself Cost Management

Because no single tool provides deep enough information, users either have logs and metrics spread across different systems or are forced to DIY solutions. Neither of these options are effective - in both instances, costs remain unattributed and bioinformaticians pay the price.  
 
In the case of the former, information has to be manually consolidated in spreadsheets and pipeline-level costs are estimated from fragmented metrics. This process is prone to error and is time-consuming. Complicating the situation further, organizations often operate across multiple AWS environments, making it even harder to assess or attribute costs.  

Many pharma and biotech teams turn to building in-house programs to fulfill their cost-monitoring needs, using product logs and billing data. These are difficult to set up and maintain.

Whether teams are using an existing platform or struggling to understand metrics across a mix of DIY and other tools, they are still experiencing cloud waste.
